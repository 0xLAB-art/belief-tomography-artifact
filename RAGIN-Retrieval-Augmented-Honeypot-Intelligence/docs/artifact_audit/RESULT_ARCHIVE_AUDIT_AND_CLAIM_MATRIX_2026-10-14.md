# RAGIN Result Archive Audit and Paper Claim Matrix

Audit snapshot: 2026-10-14, checkout `RAGIN-Retrieval-Augmented-Honeypot-Intelligence`.

## Audit boundary and coverage

The machine inventory covers every regular file below `results/` and `evidence/` at the time of the audit. It contains 2,473 files: 2,424 JSON, JSONL, or CSV files were parsed successfully; 43 readable text artifacts; and 6 binary artifacts whose bytes and SHA256 values are recorded without semantic parsing. This includes 1,472 JSON files, 939 line-oriented JSONL files, 12 CSV files, and one additional `.jsonl`-suffixed file parsed as a whole JSON document. The 2,416-line `results/laya_research/real_inference_cpu_pilot.jsonl` is that valid whole JSON document with a misleading `.jsonl` suffix; it is recorded as a suffix mismatch, not corrupt data. There are no parse failures.

The inventory records path, size, SHA256, mtime, format, path-derived family, initial status, JSON keys, and observed scalar metadata values. A total of 294 path buckets are reported in `experiment_registry.json`; these are navigation buckets, not 294 independent experiments. The manifest distinguishes parse status from scientific status. Model, producer, reference, input cohort, and parentage are left explicitly unasserted when the artifact does not encode them; filenames alone are not used to invent provenance.

The registry and claim matrix below combine direct inspection of the paper sources, archived primary artifacts, experiment protocols/manifests, and existing result ledgers/reports. Individual files that cannot be assigned confidently retain an explicit `unclassified pending artifact-level scientific review` status in the manifest. That category contains 97 files, principally mixed legacy/root-level results, model-quality outputs, and operational evidence; they are not promoted to paper claims. The pre-existing worktree is heavily modified, including manuscript, source, test, and result files. No tracked or raw result artifact was edited by this audit.

## Material manuscript-to-artifact discrepancies

1. **Verifier confidence intervals differ by paper surface and estimator.** The full paper table in `paper/sections/evaluation.tex` gives recall intervals [.920,.979], [.950,.994], and [.849,.932]. These are draft-level Wilson intervals from the earlier cross-family report. The later `results/verifier_ci_cluster_bootstrap.json` explicitly supersedes them because drafts cluster within cases; its corresponding case-cluster intervals are [.9257,.9849], [.9605,1.0000], and [.8482,.9431]. The shorter SP surface uses the clustered values (rounded) and says it resamples cases. The full paper needs the same estimator and values or a precise label that distinguishes both intervals.
2. **Evaluation hardware is inconsistent within the full manuscript.** `paper/sections/evaluation.tex` says all experiments ran on a production single host with 2 vCPU and about 4 GB RAM. `paper/sections/appendix.tex` says the offline/live evaluations used an Ubuntu workstation with 12 vCPU and 15 GiB RAM and that the deployment was separate and smaller. Reconcile this before carrying the setup claim forward.
3. **The B4 score channel is not self-describing in the raw artifact.** The manuscript calls the paired result an analysis-channel effect, but `b4_baseline_zodiac_20260922.json` stores aggregate `technique_avg`/`actor_avg` and per-row `score` without a channel field. The matching runner stores the score output but not a channel label. Preserve the measured score contrast; qualify channel attribution unless a version-bound scorer protocol/source mapping is included in the claim record.
4. **The older results ledger is stale by scope, not necessarily wrong by value.** It predates the latest Laya V2b primary, V3 adaptation, Evidence Judge and V4 studies. New paper text must use this registry and each new freeze, not treat the 2026-10-08 ledger as complete.

## Evidence Judge and Laya decision

The repository contains an implemented deterministic Evidence Judge and an opt-in filter contract. The runtime contract keeps the stage disabled by default. `LayaScoringAdapter` and the external scoring adapter are disabled placeholders without a model client, checkpoint, inference, or probability output. The synthetic 60-case result does not establish real-data accuracy. The 120-case real-data study stopped before policy, generator, verifier, human approval, commit, or final serving; the post-hoc replay changed no policy-visible set or gate. Therefore the available artifacts do not show a working end-to-end Laya-as-Jev-with-RAG arm or an evaluated decision contribution from the Evidence Judge.

These are distinct from the completed Laya V2b pre-generation decision study, the failed synthetic V3 strategy/evidence-state adaptation, and the V4 policy study. V4 uses deterministic/logistic policy arms, not Laya. None should be pooled or renamed as a single Laya+RAG result.

## Unresolved inventory and limits

- The 97 files labeled unresolved in the manifest remain explicitly outside paper claims until a producer, experiment, or archival status is established. The individual paths and hashes are listed in `artifact_manifest.jsonl`.
- Six binary artifacts are preserved by path and hash but not decoded here: two pickled policy classifiers, two PNG diagnostics, and two compressed evidence bundles. Their payload contents are not needed to substantiate any claim in the matrix without separate inspection.
- File hashes prove the observed bytes only. They do not prove an artifact's producer, execution authenticity, reference independence, or scientific validity.
- Existing result reports and ledgers were reconciled against direct artifacts for the listed claim families. This does not reconstruct every historical attempt into an independent experiment or independently rerun every metric.
- No experiments, reference annotations, model calls, or tests were run for this audit. It is a read-only result and manuscript provenance audit plus generated inventory.
