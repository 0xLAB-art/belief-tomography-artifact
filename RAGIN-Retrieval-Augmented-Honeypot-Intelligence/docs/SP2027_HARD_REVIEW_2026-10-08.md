# RAGIN: hard IEEE S&P review

**Review date:** 2026-10-08
**Target:** `paper/sp2027.tex` and its generated `paper/sp2027.pdf`
**Supporting material:** `paper/main.tex`/`paper/main.pdf`, source code, tests, results, release manifest, and results ledger
**Review standard:** IEEE S&P-style hard review, with editor-in-chief, methodology, domain/security, systems, and devil's-advocate passes.

## Recommendation

**Reject in the current submission; encourage a substantially revised resubmission.**

RAGIN has a potentially publishable systems-security idea: a retrieval-assisted honeypot can use deterministic verification, host/session fiction, canaries, and fallback transcripts to constrain model-generated deception. The implementation and artifact work are unusually serious, and several bounded claims are supported by executable evidence.

The current paper does not yet establish the stronger security and empirical claims needed for IEEE S&P. The main blockers are not prose polish. They are: an incomplete threat model; a closed, author-constructed evaluation surface; under-specified causal pairing and experiment provenance; dependence on one provider and one judge model; no independent human validation; insufficient direct baselines; and a reproduction surface that still requires guessing artifact paths and provider configuration. These issues prevent a reviewer from deciding whether the measured gains are security-relevant, generalize beyond the declared command surface, or can be independently reproduced.

This is a binary venue recommendation, not a claim that the project is unsound. The paper should preserve the deterministic safety contributions while narrowing or re-establishing the empirical claims.

## What is strong

1. The central invariant is concrete: generated content is served only after graph/identity/consistency/supported-claim checks or deterministic fallback. The five checks are cheap and deterministic, and the paper explicitly says they do not certify realism.
2. The paper distinguishes served-text quality from CTI-analysis quality in the evaluation section. This is the right conceptual separation, even though the headline framing still needs tightening.
3. The verifier evidence is useful: 105 deterministic red-team cases, zero failed-verifier serves in the recorded audit, and disclosed cross-family limits. These are bounded implementation results, not evidence of open-world security, but they are valuable.
4. The release includes row-level artifacts, bootstrap calculations, stability checks, serve provenance, a rescue benchmark, and an explicit nonclaim section. This is stronger than a typical systems-paper artifact package.
5. The authors report negative or null results: persona quality is not a clean causal win, dwell time does not improve in the small paired replay, and BM25 can outperform the graph index. This improves credibility.
6. The canonical PDF is anonymous and within the current S&P 2027 page limit. The extended 27-page PDF must remain a companion, not the submitted paper.

## Acceptance-blocking findings

### F1. The threat model is not strong enough to support the security conclusion

**Severity:** Critical
**Evidence:** `paper/sections/design.tex:159-210`; `paper/sections/sp_evaluation.tex:52-108`; `paper/sections/introduction.tex:199-204`.

The paper proves that a set of deterministic checks reject selected contradictions, identity violations, unsupported command answers, and malformed shell responses. It does not define the adversary's capabilities and objectives with enough precision to determine what security property is being claimed. Missing or underdeveloped dimensions include attacker-controlled prompt/content injection, poisoned or stale CTI, gateway/provider compromise, multi-session and multi-tenant interference, host escape, resource exhaustion, replay/adaptation against the verifier, and operator/HITL failure.

The evaluated adversarial surface is largely a closed suite designed by the authors. Forty-five re-query turns and 105 deterministic cases can establish coverage of those cases; they cannot establish resistance to an open-ended attacker. The paper itself says the supported-claim check is closed-surface and leaves open-ended recall untouched, but the abstract/contributions still invite a broader security interpretation.

**Required revision:** State a formal threat model and security property table. For each property, list attacker capability, trusted component, attack surface, evidence, and explicit nonclaim. Add held-out adversarial cases generated independently of the verifier rules, including prompt injection, CTI poisoning, cross-session contamination, malformed outputs, retry abuse, and resource pressure. If this cannot be done, narrow the security claim to fail-closed behavior on the declared command surface.

### F2. The main grounding improvement is not yet a fully auditable paired experiment

**Severity:** Critical
**Evidence:** `scripts/bootstrap_ci.py:89-103,145-178`; `scripts/run_baseline_comparison.py:92-124,138-173,190-205`; `results/b4_baseline_zodiac_20260922.json`.

The CI script computes paired differences by the string field `name`, which is a reasonable implementation choice. However, the released RAG and no-RAG arms contain the same 90 scenario names in different orders, and the artifact has no explicit pair IDs, prompt hashes, scenario-manifest hash, model/provider configuration, seed, or arm interleaving record. The baseline script runs the full arm and then the no-RAG arm with a shared session counter but does not document randomized or blocked order, deterministic scenario ordering, or a common request/configuration fingerprint.

Thus the numerical CI is computable, but the causal interpretation of the +0.1539/+0.154 overall difference is not independently auditable from the released artifact alone. The code and paper ask the reviewer to infer the pairing contract.

**Required revision:** Persist a canonical scenario ID and manifest hash in every row; assert exact set equality and uniqueness at write time; record prompt/template/config/model/provider hashes, seed, temperature, request order, and arm order; publish the paired map and a one-command pairing audit; report whether any state, cache, quota, or provider drift can cross the two arms. Re-run the comparison from that contract.

### F3. The paper's reproduction claim exceeds the current release surface

**Severity:** Critical
**Evidence:** `paper/sections/sp_appendix.tex:5-16,33-42`; `paper/sections/implementation.tex:4-6`; `paper/sections/appendix.tex:139-190`; release report provider status.

The short appendix lists artifact names such as `b4_baseline_zodiac_20260922.json` and `hitl_audit_bench.jsonl` without the `results/` prefix. From the repository root, those paths are not the files a reviewer needs to open. The implementation footnote says the repository link is omitted for review. The live protocol requires provider-backed calls, but the configured models were not available in the checked catalog and quota was not probed. Offline replay verifies deterministic components, not the live generative campaigns.

The sentence that provider-backed reruns are not required to verify submitted claims is defensible only for the explicitly archived, bounded claims. It is not equivalent to independent reproducibility of the live results.

**Required revision:** Ship a self-contained artifact bundle or a valid repository URL and release tag/commit; correct every artifact path; add a machine-readable manifest with hashes, environment, model IDs, endpoint behavior, prompt/config hashes, and expected outputs; provide an offline replay that reproduces all headline deterministic claims; and label every live number as archived evidence unless a fresh rerun is possible. Separate “artifact can be inspected” from “experiment can be independently rerun.”

### F4. The headline grounding result mixes analysis-channel and served-text capability

**Severity:** Major
**Evidence:** `paper/sections/sp_evaluation.tex:6-11,46-61`; `paper/sections/introduction.tex:107-197`; `docs/RAGIN_RESULTS_LEDGER_2026-10-08.md` two-channel section.

The raw served-text scores are approximately 0.524 for technique and 0.680 for actor, while the CTI analysis channel is much higher. The echo-guarded offline rescore reduces served technique to approximately 0.057 and actor to 0.0, while deception remains approximately 0.649. The paper acknowledges this harness-contamination issue, but the abstract-level contribution and RQ1 framing can still be read as saying that retrieval makes the honeypot serve substantially more correct TTP/actor content.

The result may still be meaningful as an analysis-channel grounding effect. It is not currently a clean live serving-quality effect.

**Required revision:** Put channel labels in every headline table, abstract sentence, figure, and contribution bullet. Make the guarded analysis the primary served-text result, or explicitly state that it is an offline rescore rather than a full guarded live rerun. Do not use the unguarded +.154 as evidence of served intelligence without this qualification.

### F5. Quality validation is a single-model, within-model-consistency study, not human validation

**Severity:** Major
**Evidence:** `paper/sections/sp_evaluation.tex:110-119,181-183`; `paper/sections/introduction.tex:112-148`; `paper/sections/sp_appendix.tex:18-24`.

The 3.86/5 consensus result uses three evaluator personas of one judge model; the judge shares a provider with the generator. The paper correctly says this is within-model consistency and not a human study. That limitation is nevertheless material because realism, plausibility, and deception are the central quality outcomes. No blinded human raters, inter-rater reliability, judge-model sensitivity, or independent non-LLM audit establishes that the judge's score tracks an operator's judgment.

**Required revision:** Add blinded human evaluation with an independently specified rubric and inter-rater agreement, or demote the quality result to an internal diagnostic and remove any external deception-quality conclusion. At minimum, run multiple independent judge models/providers and report sensitivity, with generator and judge separated where possible.

### F6. Baselines and related work are not sufficient for an S&P novelty claim

**Severity:** Major
**Evidence:** evaluation baseline tables and related-work discussion; `paper/sections/sp_evaluation.tex:125-183`.

The comparison set is mainly Vanilla Cowrie/stateful LLM/bare LLM/RAGIN plus internal ablations. That does not establish superiority or novelty relative to recent generative honeypots, LLM-in-shell systems, adaptive deception systems, runtime policy enforcement, prompt-injection defenses, or verifier/guardrail approaches. Claims equivalent to “the industry answer is silence” are broader than the evidence shown.

**Required revision:** Add a systematic comparison matrix and direct baselines where feasible, including recent generative honeypot and runtime-enforcement systems. If direct reproduction is impossible, narrow the novelty claim to the particular combination of retrieval, deterministic verification, host fiction, and fallback, and explain the non-comparable dimensions.

### F7. The adaptive/intelligence-ladder evidence does not establish adaptive security benefit

**Severity:** Major
**Evidence:** `paper/sections/sp_evaluation.tex:156-183`; rescue artifact and JEV artifacts.

The paper reports 12/13 log replay, a 200-session routing ceiling, a six-row hard subset with one rescue and five refusals, and a five-pair bait replay with +125% exposure but no dwell-time improvement. The held-out 400-session rescue benchmark is explicitly provider-free and self-referential: its gold risk is derived from the same controller floor. These are useful diagnostics, but they do not show that the ladder improves attacker engagement, detection, analyst utility, or security outcomes under realistic adaptive traffic.

**Required revision:** Recast this as exploratory controller validation, remove it from the central effectiveness argument, or add multi-turn attacker goals, realistic traffic, independent labels, and a pre-specified security utility metric. Do not treat self-consistency against the controller's own floor as independent effectiveness evidence.

### F8. External validity is too narrow for the operational security claims

**Severity:** Major
**Evidence:** `paper/sections/implementation.tex:58-69`; `paper/sections/introduction.tex:199-204`; `paper/sections/sp_evaluation.tex:156-183`.

The deployment is tailnet-only, the live campaign is small and author-controlled, the field log sample is 13 sessions, the bait replay has five pairs, and the consensus set has eight scenarios. There is no ambient attacker traffic, multi-tenant deployment, cross-provider evaluation, long-running cost/latency characterization, or evidence that the measured policy changes an operational defender's outcome. The paper acknowledges some of this, but the security framing still reads more broadly than the external validity warrants.

**Required revision:** Add a claim-boundary table distinguishing proven, supported-but-limited, exploratory, and untested properties. Report the deployment scope as a first-class limitation and make “safe on the evaluated closed surface” the operational conclusion unless broader evidence is added.

## Additional major findings

### F9. The full companion contains stale CI and validation claims

**Severity:** Major for the full-paper package; moderate for the short submission.
**Evidence:** `paper/sections/appendix.tex:129-137` claims zero-error full-tree type checking, three Python-version test runs, and secret/static/dependency scans. The current release report records pre-existing Ruff findings, five mypy errors, 30 Bandit warnings, and unavailable dependency scanners.

The short paper should not rely on the companion's stronger historical wording. Correct or label it as historical, and distinguish “tests passed in this environment” from “all CI gates are clean.”

### F10. Artifact paths in the canonical appendix are incorrect relative to the repository root

**Severity:** Major for artifact evaluation; easy to fix.
**Evidence:** `paper/sections/sp_appendix.tex:5-16,33-42`.

The listed JSON/JSONL names resolve under `results/`, not at the root as written. This is a concrete reviewer friction and reproducibility defect, not merely a documentation preference. Correct the paths and add a path-resolution test.

## Minor findings

1. “Two independent 218-query campaigns” needs a definition of independence. The artifacts should state whether model, provider, prompt, seed, scenario order, evaluator, and cache state were independent. Until then, call them “two reruns.”
2. The latency-derived verified/deterministic split should not be presented as a direct path count unless it comes from `serve_provenance.json`; clearly distinguish measured provenance from inference from latency.
3. The paper should report the exact number of unique scenarios per suite and the construction/selection rule. Repeated rows from a fixed author-created suite are not independent attacker encounters.
4. The random-forest profiler needs training/test split provenance, class balance, and leakage checks if its classifications are part of the security argument.
5. CTI poisoning resistance is asserted at the implementation level, but no poisoning attack evaluation is shown. Either add one or narrow the claim to screening/quarantine mechanisms.
6. The cost/latency section should report provider failures, retries, fallback rates, tail latency, and per-session cost, not only average response time and derived path shares.
7. The repository is dirty with generated session data, PDF/source edits, and `bandit-report.json`. The release manifest should specify exactly which files constitute the version of record and exclude scratch/generated data that are not evidence.
8. The current S&P 2027 CFP permits up to 13 pages of text plus up to 5 pages of references/appendices, total at most 18 pages, and requires anonymity. The 11-page `sp2027.pdf` passes this format check; the 27-page `main.pdf` does not represent a submission-ready format.

## Reviewer-panel synthesis

### Editor-in-chief pass

The paper has a coherent problem and a plausible systems contribution, but the evidence does not yet support the breadth of the security conclusion. Multiple acceptance blockers require new experiments or substantial claim reduction. Recommendation: reject current submission, invite resubmission after evidence closure.

### Methodology pass

The largest methodological issue is not the bootstrap implementation itself; it is the missing experimental contract around the paired arms. The second is the mismatch between the unit of analysis (fixed scenarios/rows) and the implied unit of generalization (attackers and operational sessions). The paper needs explicit estimands, pair IDs, sampling/construction rules, and independence assumptions.

### Domain/security pass

The verifier/fallback design is security-relevant, but security requires an adversary model and attack evaluation. The current tests mostly show that the authors can enforce selected invariants. They do not establish resistance to adaptive attackers, poisoning, cross-session attacks, resource abuse, or deployment compromise.

### Systems pass

The architecture is credible and the offline test discipline is a strength. However, provider availability, gateway dependence, single-host/tailnet deployment, missing model/config metadata, and absent operational traffic measurements prevent a systems-security claim about deployability or robustness.

### Devil's-advocate pass

The most dangerous alternative explanation is that retrieval increases CTI labels in the analysis channel while the apparent serving improvement is partly scorer contamination and fixed-suite memorization. A second is that deterministic fallback/verifier rules, rather than retrieval, carry most of the safety result. The paper should make these hypotheses explicit and test them directly.

## Required revision roadmap

### P0: required before another S&P submission

1. Freeze and publish the threat model, trusted computing base, attacker capabilities, and property/evidence/nonclaim matrix.
2. Repair the paired experiment contract: canonical IDs, manifest/config/model hashes, order/seed/state metadata, exact pair audit, and a clean rerun or a formally bounded archived analysis.
3. Correct artifact paths, publish the repository/tag or self-contained bundle, and add a manifest-driven offline replay for every headline deterministic result.
4. Reframe all raw/echo-guarded/channel-specific numbers in the abstract, claims, tables, and conclusion. Do not present unguarded serving scores as clean served capability.
5. Add independent quality validation or demote the judge result to an internal diagnostic.
6. Add stronger direct baselines or narrow the novelty and related-work claims.

### P1: required to support broader security significance

1. Evaluate held-out, independently constructed adaptive attacks, including injection, poisoning, cross-session drift, retry/resource abuse, and malformed tool output.
2. Add realistic multi-turn traffic and an operational security utility metric; report cost, failure, retry, fallback, and tail-latency distributions.
3. Evaluate at least one additional provider/model family or make provider dependence a formal limitation and avoid cross-provider generalization claims.
4. Clean the full companion's stale CI claims and make the release manifest identify the exact version of record.

### P2: clarity and presentation

1. Replace “independent campaigns” with a provenance-backed term.
2. Add exact artifact paths and a one-command audit entry point.
3. Separate the 11-page anonymous S&P paper from the 27-page extended audit companion.
4. Retain the negative results and explicitly label the rescue benchmark as self-consistency.

## Final verdict

RAGIN is a promising, unusually well-instrumented prototype with a credible deterministic safety mechanism. The current paper is not ready for IEEE S&P because it overreaches from closed-surface software checks and archived single-provider evaluations to broader security and effectiveness conclusions. The most credible path is to make the verifier/fallback invariant the primary contribution, close the threat-model and provenance gaps, obtain independent quality/security evidence, and narrow every claim that cannot be freshly or independently reproduced.

**Decision:** Reject / resubmit after major revision.
**Confidence:** High on the identified acceptance blockers; moderate on the ultimate publishability after revision.
