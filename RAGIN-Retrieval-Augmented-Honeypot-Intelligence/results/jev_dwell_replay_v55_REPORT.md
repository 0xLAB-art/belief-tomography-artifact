# Jev dwell replay — v5.5 (gateway-recovery re-run)

**Question:** did the v5.5 mechanisms (ambient dynamism, terminal-grammar lint, canary-bound IMDS credentials) kill the v5 tells on live attacker-LLM traffic?

**Answer: partially — with a major methodology break that must be stated first.**

## Methodology break: self-play

Between v5 and v5.5, the apmix free gateway **pruned its catalog to exactly one model** (`deepseek-v4.1-flash-free`, verified via `/v1/models`). Both `gemini-2.5-flash-free` (defender) and the older `deepseek` attacker IDs now 404. Consequences:

- **Attacker and defender are now the same weights** (self-play). Any treated-vs-baseline delta is confounded: the "attacker" reasons about a fiction generated by the same model family. Cross-version comparisons and the A/B delta are **directional only**.
- The new model is a **reasoning model** (~1–2k hidden COT tokens, ~24 s/call measured). The replay script was hardened for this: `llm()` default `max_tokens` 220 → 4096, timeout 180 s, `content=None` guarded, and the script now checks the **live gateway catalog** at startup and switches to self-play explicitly instead of failing canary (which it did, twice, before the fix).
- `.env`'s `RAGIN_HISOKA_MODEL` repointed to the surviving model; artifact `method` strings now record self-play honestly.

## Three NEW tells the attacker found (all real, all actionable)

1. **`AK7X…` key prefix** — we deliberately avoided `AKIA`/`ASIA` so fake keys wouldn't match our own scrubber patterns; the attacker-LLM knows real AWS prefixes and flags anything else. The fix (next round): use real-shaped `AKIA`/`ASIA` prefixes and scope the scrubber's secret patterns to *not* fire on our own deterministic IMDS bodies (they're generated, not LLM-leaked).
2. **Mid-session template fallback breaks fiction** — the treated lateral session had 1 template call (LLM refused twice); its leave reason was "inconsistent responses". A template reply interleaved with LLM replies is itself a consistency breaker. Fix candidates: style-match templates to the LLM's transcript shape, or treat a double-refusal as a signal to end the session gracefully rather than degrade.
3. **Sudo fiction contradiction** — cloud baseline: `id` showed the user in the `sudo` group, but sudo attempts granted nothing. Identity facts and authorization fiction must agree (a ledger fact-class problem: "user is in group X" must imply command policy).

## Honesty box

- n=4, single seed, **self-play** — nothing here is statistically meaningful; the A/B delta especially.
- Both arms ran the Jev-guided vs baseline conditions as before, but with identical weights the treated arm's guidance advantage is conceptually muddied.
- Attacker-LLM latency (~24 s/turn, plus Hisoka's own reasoning calls) makes large-n runs expensive on the free tier; expect ~15–20 min per 4-session batch.

## Net assessment

The v5.5 mechanisms did their specific jobs (no patterned-key or static-listing leaves), but this round's value is mostly **negative results + three precise new tells**, delivered under a compromised methodology. The credential-prefix, template-fallback, and sudo-consistency fixes are well-scoped next round; a second free model on the gateway would restore a valid A/B.
